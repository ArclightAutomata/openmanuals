Your identity (optional) Off

You only need an identity to contribute back to OpenManuals. When you download a manual that isn't in the database yet, or report a bad copy, the extension tells the OpenManuals server about it, and those notes are signed by your identity. Without one, finding manuals and downloading them from archive.org work the same, and with upload enabled your files still go to archive.org, but nothing is recorded on the OpenManuals server and you get no credit.

OpenManuals has no accounts. Your identity is a signing key made by this extension and protected by a passkey. You can choose to link this key to other identities you own, like on GitHub, Mastodon, your domain, etc. Linked identities that check out add trust: others can see who stands behind your contributions, and your reports carry more weight. The private key leaves this browser only encrypted, so that just your passkey can open it.

Your key is protected by a passkey from your password manager, phone or security key. On another computer, sign in with the same passkey. No password to remember, nothing to download.

Advanced options

Create a key with a backup file

The key is also saved to a file encrypted with a passphrase. The file and passphrase restore it without a passkey, for example if you lose every passkey.

Restore from a backup file

With its passphrase. Or choose the file and click “Sign in with passkey” above, if the server doesn't have the passkey's copy.

Passkeys

Any of these passkeys signs you in on another computer. Each one keeps an encrypted copy of the key, here and on the server; only that passkey can open it.

    Cropped to a square and resized to 256×256 in this browser before it's sent (which also drops photo location data). It's published with your profile; the image itself is kept on the server, not in the public ledger, and replaced when you change it.

    Proofs

    Each proof is a place you control that contains your identifier. Add as many as you like. A proof that checks out is added to your profile and published right away; anyone (and keyoxide.org) can check them.
